For Ibrahem is a piece about a Palestinian man I once knew. In the years 2024 and 2025, I was attempting to support him and his family by interacting with him on the social media platform TikTok as a way of bringing his account to the forefront of the website's algorithm. We communicated a few times through direct messaging, but I largely learned about his everyday life through videos he would post documenting the conditions of those living in Palestine. Ibrahem himself was a resident of Palestine at the time I knew him, and I am unaware of his whereabouts now. Near the end of January of 2025, I stopped receiving messages from Ibrahem. I have since attempted to reach out to him, hoping to find a way that I could support him or at least maintain contact. I have not heard from Ibrahem since July 24th, 2025. The Palestinian Information Center (PIC) marks that day as the 657th day of the Israeli occupation on Gaza. PIC reported that at least 20 palestinians were killed by air strikes that day. I may never know if Ibrahem is still alive with his father, Adham; his mother, Amani; his sisters, Miral and Dima; or his wife and two daughters. 

At the time I am writing this on November 13th, 2026, it has since been 1,133 days since October 7th of 2023. Thousands of cases such as Ibrahem's have been witnessed by countless people around the world as strikes upon Gaza continue. For Ibrahem attempts to convey the dissonance and uncertainty I experienced in witnessing Ibrahem's struggles as well as the Palestinian genocide as a whole.

	It is intensely that I must express how important communication between the laptopist and pianist is within this piece. If possible, performers should take time before the piece to outline a few gestures which they will use to communicate emotions such as "stress", "relief", "evil" and "shame". I relinquish these emotional gestures to the performers to ensure that their voices may find place within this piece. These emotions should be mapped onto the respective scores of the performers, such that segments of the score are understood to describe the emotions evoked within them by the material. Performers will thus navigate the score in a "stream of consciousness" fashion, where their figurative and gestural dialogue will be the driving external factor which steers their journey. Notes for Pianist:
	The pianist is presented with a collage musical materials. It includes passages for one or two hands, although sections physically requiring two hands may be modified so that only a selection of it may be played with one (i.e. the left hand part mustn't be strictly performed alongside the right.). This "collage" is truly not a full collage, more so a visual grouping of phrases which may be connected in any way. There are dotted arrows to provide suggested paths documented by the composer (myself), but no one needs to listen to what I say. As mentioned previously, the pianist may "split" where they are playing more than one phrase at a time. There are no strict guidelines to the nature of this splitting, but I suggest that the performer curate lines or motives from various sections and performs them simultaneously.
	Pianist, please note that the computer element of this piece does enact extensions of your performance. For example, the one bar phrase referred to as "theta" has special note heads on a few pitches that are marked, only play the "regular" noteheads, the computer will recognize them and play these extensions for you, and potentially embellish upon them. I currently refrain from noting any further computer interactions you may or may not have due to the fact that I have not yet written them at this time.
